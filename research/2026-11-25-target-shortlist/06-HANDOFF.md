# Revised local irregularity handoff

The target is the revised three-colour conjecture for connected decomposable simple graphs, with the bow-tie cactus exception. The unrestricted target is open. See the [research note](06-revised-local-irregularity.md) for proofs and primary sources.

## Second session, 2026-11-25: subdivision frames

Outcome: there is no resolution and no counterexample. The session proves a new sufficient condition, the frame lemma, and states a sharper conjecture for subcubic graphs with exhaustive evidence through order 13. It also completes the independent verification of the rank-three certificates. That yields two computer-assisted partial theorems: one for a single noncactus block of rank at most three, and one for subcubic block structures. Details are in [the note](06-revised-local-irregularity.md#subdivision-frames-for-subcubic-graphs-second-session-2026-11-25).

- **Target choice.** The subcubic case is Conjecture 6.1 of arXiv 2210.04649. The cubic case is also open. Lužar, Przybyło and Soták proved the bound four. The bow-tie is not subcubic, so a proof for subcubic graphs would be a natural strong partial theorem.
- **Proved.** Lemma 2 in the note: a *frame* yields an explicit locally irregular 3-edge-colouring. A frame consists of an independent set $Y$ of degree-2 or degree-3 vertices, with one designated $S$-edge at each degree-3 member, such that $S$ is locally irregular and the multigraph $H$ satisfies parity condition (F2). Lemma 1 proves the parity condition for $H$ by spanning-tree propagation. The proof is elementary. It does not rely on the sketched multigraph theorem of arXiv 1611.02341.
- **Conjecture with evidence.** Every connected decomposable subcubic graph except $K_4$ has a frame, and this would imply Conjecture 6.1. The exhaustive check covers all 96,847 connected subcubic graphs through order 13. The counts match OEIS A243393. The only 3-colourable graph without a frame is $K_4$, and the graphs with no 3-colouring are nondecomposable cacti. Every frame was converted to a colouring and checked independently. Random cubic graphs of orders 14 through 70 all have frames. Forest frames, where $H$ is acyclic, have more exceptions: $W_5$, $B_{10}$ and chains or cycles of copies of $K_{2,3}$.
- **Obstruction.** No inductive proof was found. Natural strengthenings fail in small graphs: forcing degree-2 vertices into $Y$, forcing low-degree vertices into $X$, and requiring designated vertices to absorb all of their $Y$-neighbours. Single-vertex and edge deletions are not universally extendable, because adding an edge at an $X$-vertex changes its $S$-degree. The direct approach uses a maximal independent $Y$ and an induced linear forest $G[X]$. It leaves an exact-multiplicity assignment problem with alternating demands along the paths, and there is no Hall-type proof yet.
- **Rank-three certificates are independently verified.** `06-handoff/verify-kernel-cover.py` completed, with the receipt saved as `06-handoff/verify-kernel-cover-receipt.txt`. Two consequences follow. A graph whose only noncactus block has cycle rank at most three, with arbitrary cactus attachments, is 3-colourable. So is a decomposable subcubic graph in which every noncactus block has rank at most three or no $K_4$ minor. Both are computer-assisted, and their statements and proofs are in the note, after the rank-three section.
- **Third pass on the cubic case.** By Catlin–Lai near-bipartiteness, every connected cubic graph other than $K_4$ has a maximal independent $Y$ with $G-Y$ an induced linear forest (an MIL partition). The cubic frame conjecture therefore reduces to finding designations for a suitable MIL partition. Such designations exist for every cubic graph of order at most 12 except the Petersen graph. Extremal choices of the partition are not universal at order 12, and no Hall-type proof was found. A complete reducible-configuration proof would need forall-exists checks over radius-three neighbourhoods; this is infeasible as it stands. Details are in the note's "Third pass" section. The cubic case remains open.
- **Fourth pass on route 1, where the session stopped.** Lemma 1′ in the note gives a complete proof that (F2) may be weakened to (F2w), which only requires that no component of $H$ be an odd cycle. The proof colours threads in two modes. Under (F2w) every parity-only failure disappears. Exhaustively through order 12, every connected cubic graph except $K_4$ and Petersen has a feasible MIL partition. Every maximum-size MIL partition is feasible, except in the Petersen graph and one order-12 graph, whose adjacency is listed in the note. Random cubic samples at orders 14 to 20 found no infeasible maximum-size MIL partition. The remaining gap is a single **designation lemma**. It asks for an MIL partition $(Y,X)$ of a connected cubic graph other than $K_4$ and Petersen, together with a map $s:Y\to X$ along edges, satisfying three conditions. Along each path of $G[X]$ the values $r-p$ alternate, where $r$ counts $Y$-neighbours and $p$ counts designations; they lie in $\{0,1\}$ at interior vertices and $\{0,1,2\}$ at ends. An isolated vertex of $G[X]$ receives a number of designations other than one. Finally (F2w) holds. The data suggest proving it for maximum-size MIL partitions, with an explicit exchange to handle the order-12 exception, and treating Petersen separately: it has a frame with non-maximal $Y$ of size 3. Coordinator plan items 1–3 are done in this sense. Item 2's potential-function proof was not found. For item 4, no clean natural subclass with a complete proof was identified. Bipartite cubic graphs already have $\chi'_{\mathrm{irr}}\le2$ by known results. The cubic case remains open.
- **Fifth pass (third session, in progress).** Lemma M is proved: for a maximum MIL partition, if $A\subseteq X$ is independent, $N(A)\cap Y\subseteq R$, and every $y\in R$ has two neighbours in $A$, then $|A|\le|R|$. It implies that no $y$ has two nonadjacent interior neighbours, so forced designations never conflict, and every $K_2$-end has a free neighbour. For triangle-free graphs, designations reduce to an *absorption lemma*, and the sufficiency direction is proved. In that lemma, isolated vertices absorb 0, 2 or 3 free neighbours, $K_2$-components absorb exactly one, and W-ends absorb pairs. Every deficient tree component of $\Gamma_U$ must be hit. A new count-verified generator covers all cubic graphs through order 14. Every maximum MIL partition is feasible except in Petersen and the order-12 graph. The absorption model, with even-path phases chosen to minimise the deficient size, works for all triangle-free graphs of orders 12 and 14. The proof of the absorption lemma is open; the planned route is the Tutte–Gallai covering-matching criterion combined with Lemma M. (F2w) and the triangle case are also open. Details are in the note's Fifth pass section.
- **Most promising next step (updated, third session).** Prove the *absorption lemma* for triangle-free cubic graphs. Fix a maximum MIL partition, put phase A on odd paths, and choose the even-path phases to minimise the total size of the deficient $\Gamma_U$-components. The lemma asks for an assignment in which every deficient component has a vertex absorbed. Isolated vertices absorb 0, 2 or 3 free neighbours, each $K_2$ absorbs an odd number, W-ends absorb pairs, and absorbed vertices are distinct. Computationally this holds for every triangle-free cubic graph of order 12 or 14, and fails at order 10 only for Petersen and for one partition of one graph. Suggested proof: treat isolated-vertex and W pairs as matching edges, apply the Tutte–Gallai covering-matching criterion, and turn each barrier into a pair $(R,A)$ violating Lemma M. The note gives the counting for barriers with $S=\emptyset$. Missing pieces: independence corrections, barriers with $S
eq\emptyset$, the role of the phase rule, (F2w) (rarely binding: 5 of 723 first solutions), and triangles. Triangles need extra $K_2$ modes, or a triangle-contraction lemma. With all of these, the cubic case of Conjecture 6.1 follows for triangle-free graphs; the full cubic case then needs triangles. Tools: `06-handoff/mil_sat.py` (maximum MIL partitions and designations by SAT), `mil_structure.py` (classification, deficient components, absorption SAT), `mil_rules.py`, `mil_survey.py` and `cubic_gen.py`. All need `python-sat` and `networkx` installed with `pip install --target <scratch>` on `PYTHONPATH`. Also `lir_sat.py`, a plain locally irregular colouring SAT with star-forest restrictions.

## Completed proofs and finite certificates

- For a degree-two connector, let a bit record whether consecutive edge colours agree. At each internal edge the adjacent bits must sum to one, so they alternate. The two terminal degrees are then one or two. The table in the research note gives the complete endpoint-colour and endpoint-degree relations. Equality classes of runs give the short-length exceptions.
- The normalized kernel search gives all noncacti of minimum degree at least two and cycle rank at most four. The independent Python enumeration checked 87 kernels and 1,781,720 length assignments. A separate implementation verified all 895 noncactus rank-five kernels with 3,957,285 state witnesses. Only one implementation was recorded for the rank-five computation.
- Every connected simple noncactus graph with a genuine two-edge cut into two connected cacti is three-colourable. The proof handles arbitrary shared articulation vertices, nested cycles and branches. It normalizes side attachments to bounded terminal colour-count conditions, scans bounded junctions, and closes the side relations under bridge composition. The saved checker reproduced 124 intermediate states, 24 traversed relations and 61 final relations after 8,198 bridge compositions. Noncactus status records a traversed cycle on at least one side of the cut.
- Reducing the acceptance relation for a fixed exterior colouring gives small representatives for a cactus side in a vertex-minimal counterexample. The side has at most nine vertices for distinct terminals and thirteen for coincident terminals. The bridge-side bound is five, and the argument in the note isolates the rooted half-bow-tie as the only exceptional five-vertex case in the sharper one-port analysis.
- If the only noncactus block consists of at least three internally disjoint paths between two vertices, arbitrary cactus attachments are allowed. The finite checks cover 216,832 cases with three paths and 2,025,366 with four. The proof for five or more uses endpoint colour counts for the subdivided connectors and reserves a colour for the single possible direct edge.
- The research note gives larger subdivided-core classes with arbitrary cactus attachments. The minimum kernel-degree bounds are six, seven and ten when the direct edges are absent, form a matching or form a pseudoforest, respectively.

## Final verification and scope

- The latest standalone cactus checker exited successfully and printed `all two-ended cactus connectors use the same seven arc modes`. It checked equality of the entire sets of acceptance masks, using both relative color orbits and exterior degree 20 as the sentinel for every degree above 19. No independent low-rank kernel certificate is needed for the $K_4$-minor-free consequence.
- The umbrella theorem for subcubic $K_4$-minor-free graphs is now written in [the research note](06-revised-local-irregularity.md#subcubic-graphs-without-a-k4-minor). For an initial noncactus block, suppress degree-two vertices to get a loopless bridgeless cubic kernel. In a kernel larger than the two-vertex theta, a double edge has two distinct exterior neighbors. A common exterior neighbor would have exactly one edge out of that three-vertex set, making that edge a bridge. The doubled routes, their tails and their rooted ornaments form a connected cactus. Suppress the piece using one of seven acceptance-minimal modes, each realized by a simple path with private rooted cactus attachments. This preserves the exterior incidence colors and degrees when lifting. The reduced bridgeless cubic kernel represents another noncactus block, and its underlying simple graph is a minor of the previous one. Dirac's minimum-degree theorem excludes a simple cubic kernel in a $K_4$-minor-free graph, so the reduction ends at the parallel-path theta. [Dirac theorem reference](https://users.monash.edu.au/~davidwo/papers/FijWoo-EJC10.pdf)
- To combine blocks in a subcubic graph, nontrivial blocks cannot share a vertex, so the relevant pieces are linked by bridges. Process noncactus blocks from the leaves toward a chosen root block. At a child's attachment the port degrees lie in $\{1,2,3\}$. Attaching each exact forced-degree cactus probe in turn creates a graph with a single noncactus block. The one-block theorem then shows that the child can avoid each of the three possible exterior degrees, so it has at least two realizable port degrees. A leaf substitution lifts by matching its bridge color and choosing a distinct degree. Starting with the furthest noncactus block ensures that every ornament at the next step is already cactus.
- In a vertex-minimal counterexample a bridge cactus side has at most two vertices unless it is the five-vertex rooted half of the original bow-tie. The mirror proof uses the known nondecomposable cactus family. With a port on one of its vertex-disjoint triangles, every even pendant path and odd route to a child triangle prevents degree two at the parent end. Induction around the triangle then forces degree two at its port. The original bow-tie half is the unique rooted cactus signature $\{3\}$. For a genuine two-edge cut into two noncacti, coincident ports at a degree-three vertex are separately ruled out by joining each side to a fresh single edge. The witness modes at the coincident root are $(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$ with distinct colors, and port order symmetry gives both.
- The private-block variant in `private-cactus-petals.py` passed all 53 distinct-terminal cactus relations. With the exterior terminal merged into one root, the relation with freely chosen spoke order contains the private-triangle or private-square relation. As a consequence, a theta block attached through one of its degree-two vertices can be handled this way, because removal of that vertex from the block leaves a cactus. The main proof above does not require this extra verification.

## Cycle rank three with arbitrary cactus attachments

A unique noncactus block with cycle rank at most three reduces to one of the following cases. Degree-two suppression of a 2-connected noncactus block has no loops. At rank $r$ its kernel has $m=n+r-1$, with $2m\ge3n$, so rank three permits at most four vertices. At two vertices the paths are parallel. At three vertices, the degrees are $4,3,3$ and uniquely force a triangle with the two sides incident with its degree-four vertex doubled. At four vertices all degrees are three. A doubled pair must have different outside neighbors, after which the other pair is doubled. In the absence of a doubled pair the kernel is $K_4$.
